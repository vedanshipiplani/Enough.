✦ What is Parental Pressure?
Understanding what's truly going on.
Your grades don't define you, yet someone forgot to tell our parents that. Were you aware that when 32 students at DVHS took a survey, 50% of the participants described that their parents or guardians have high academic expectations and that good grades are expected. The problem here is how overcontrolling parents or guardians with high academic expectations in the DVHS community affect students' stress levels, causing them to have increased mental health struggles. It's important we take action because parental stress is increasing daily and the students carrying that weight deserve to be heard, understood, and supported.

"I wish they knew how much pressure can really affect you and how pressure can lead to mental health struggles."

— Anonymous DVHS student
✦ About This Project
Why we decided it was time to make change
We noticed the increased amounts of stress in students not only coming from school, but also at home. Home should be a kid's safe space and it's important we keep it that way for them. Parents believe that being overcontrolling can help their kids but in reality, it's doing quite the opposite. It was time we took action over the situation, and did our best to try and help!
Our ultimate goal is to shift the mental models parents hold about what "good parenting" actually looks like, especially academically. Most parents measure their support by how much they push their child, but rather they should just set guidelines. It's important to let your child know it's okay to struggle, fail, get up and try again. The shift from pressure to presence is what we are fighting for in this project.

✦ For Parents
Actions speak louder than expectations.
Actions as a parent or guardian are made with the best intentions for their student. Sometimes these decisions can subconsciously negatively impact your student mentally. Below are some actions you can take to help your student in the best way.
🌙

Attend Parent Information Nights

These are very influential as they can provide information about the upcoming year for your student and you can learn about your child's options — helping you support them with full context rather than assumptions.

🏊

Act as a Lifeguard

This strategy allows your student to experiment and make decisions for themselves. If something goes wrong, you as a guardian will be there to help — present and ready, but not hovering over every wave.
